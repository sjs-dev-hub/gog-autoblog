The tempting listing is often the one with one good photo and a vague condition label. Before you chase the lowest price, make the seller show you the details that determine whether the driver actually fits your bag and your next tee shot.

## The quick verdict

Buy a used driver online only when the listing lets you identify the exact club, evaluate its visible condition, and confirm the setup matches your needs. A lower price is not useful if key details remain unanswered.

## Start with the driver you are replacing, not the listing price

A used driver can look like a bargain until it arrives with the wrong handedness, an unfamiliar loft, or a shaft setup you did not mean to buy. Begin with your current driver or the setup you already know you want: handedness, approximate loft, shaft flex or model if known, and any length preference. Those details turn a broad search into a relevant one.

**Guild recommendation:** Write your non-negotiables before opening listings. If a listing does not state one of them, treat that as a question to resolve rather than an assumption to make.

## Identify the exact club before judging condition

A listing title may be abbreviated, while the photos and description carry the meaningful details. Match the stated model, loft, handedness, shaft information, and included components across the listing. If the club has an adjustable setting system, ask which setting is shown and whether any stated wrench or headcover is included. Do not infer included items from a photo alone when the description is silent.

**Guild recommendation:** Save the listing only after you can summarize the exact club in one sentence, including its orientation, stated loft, and shaft setup.

## Demand photos that answer a condition question

One angled image can make a driver look cleaner than it is, but a set of close, separate views lets you assess what is actually visible. Ask for clear photos of the crown, face, sole, shaft, grip, and the area where the head and shaft meet. Compare like with like: a listing with detailed images may be easier to evaluate than a cheaper listing that leaves major areas unseen.

**Guild recommendation:** Pass when a visible area matters to your decision and the seller will not clarify it with a photo or a direct answer.

## Read condition language as a prompt for follow-up

Words such as excellent, clean, or lightly used are not a substitute for a description of the club in the listing. The FTC states that advertising claims should be truthful, non-deceptive, and supported. Use that standard as a practical reading tool: look for specific statements that match the photos, then ask concise questions where the listing is vague.

**Guild recommendation:** Ask one focused message: request confirmation of the club’s stated configuration, whether there is damage the seller has not shown, and whether the pictured club is the club being sold.

## Check conformance when it matters to your play

If you intend to use the driver in play where conformance is relevant, use the USGA Informational Club Database to check driver conformance information. This check addresses conformance information, not the club’s physical condition, fit, authenticity, or suitability for your swing. Keep those decisions separate.

**Guild recommendation:** Search the database using the clearest identifying information from the listing, and retain the listing details that support your match.

## Compare uncertainty, not just the asking price

Two listings that appear similar can carry very different amounts of unanswered risk. One may show every important area and state its setup clearly; another may be cheaper but omit the loft, shaft details, or close condition photos. Put those differences beside the price before deciding. The strongest value is the listing whose remaining unknowns you can accept.

**Guild recommendation:** Use the same checklist on two or three candidates, then choose the club you can describe most confidently rather than the one with the most attractive headline price.

## Search only after you know what to compare

Once you have established your handedness, loft range, and shaft preference, a focused search can reduce irrelevant results. Search terms should narrow the starting field, not override the individual listing. Apply the photo, configuration, condition, and conformance checks again after you find a candidate.

**Guild recommendation:** Use the searches below as starting points, then reject any result that fails your written checklist.

## The Five-Minute Listing Audit

Open one used-driver listing beside a blank note or checklist. Do not compare prices until you have completed the same review for each candidate.

<ol>

<li>Write the club’s handedness, stated loft, stated shaft model or flex, stated length if provided, and included accessories if stated.</li>
<li>Look for separate, well-lit views of the crown, face, sole, shaft, grip, and head connection area. Mark each area as clearly shown, partly shown, or not shown.</li>
<li>Read the description for a specific condition statement rather than relying only on a broad label such as good or used.</li>
<li>List every unanswered question in one short seller message, including any missing setup detail or unclear area in the photos.</li>
<li>Compare only listings that have enough information for you to explain why one suits your needs better than the other.</li>

</ol>

## The practical takeaway

- Know the setup you want before comparing used-driver listings.
- Require enough photos and description detail to evaluate the club’s visible condition and stated configuration.
- Ask direct questions instead of filling gaps with assumptions.
- Use the USGA Informational Club Database for conformance information when that matters to your intended play.
- Choose the listing with the clearest evidence and acceptable uncertainty, not simply the lowest price.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should I ask a seller before buying a used driver online?

Ask the seller to confirm the exact handedness, stated loft, shaft information, and included items. Request clear photos of any area that is missing or unclear in the listing, and ask whether the pictured club is the club being sold.

### Can the USGA database tell me whether a used driver is in good condition?

No. The USGA Informational Club Database can be used to check driver conformance information. Visible condition and configuration still need to be assessed from the listing, photos, and seller responses.

### Is a broad condition label enough to buy from?

No. Treat a broad condition label as a starting point, then look for specific description and photos that support it. Ask for clarification when the evidence does not answer a decision-relevant question.

### When should I pass on a used-driver listing?

Pass when you cannot confirm the club’s essential setup, when important areas are not shown clearly, or when the seller’s answers leave you unable to explain what you are buying.
